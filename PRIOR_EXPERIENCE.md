# Prior Listening and Ear-Training Experience

## Starting Context

This project does not represent my first exposure to ear training or critical listening. It marks the beginning of a more systematic phase in which technical-listening performance is practiced through a defined curriculum, measured over time, and documented alongside recurring errors and engineering interpretation.

The baseline scores recorded in this repository should therefore be understood as the starting point for this specific software-based curriculum—not as the beginning of my overall listening development.

## Formal Musical Training

I completed undergraduate degrees in Jazz Performance and Music Education. That education included foundational music theory and aural-skills training as well as sustained ensemble and performance experience.

This background developed experience with:

- Pitch, interval, chord, and rhythmic recognition
- Instrumental timbre and ensemble balance
- Intonation
- Musical phrasing and articulation
- Listening within live ensemble contexts
- Translating an audible result into musical language

## Audio Engineering and Critical Listening

I later completed a Master of Science in Audio Engineering Technology at Belmont University. Graduate study and production work expanded listening from primarily musical judgments toward recording, processing, reproduction, and technical evaluation.

Relevant experience included:

- Recording, editing, and mixing
- Microphone selection and placement
- Signal flow and monitoring
- Equalization and dynamics processing
- Stereo imaging, localization, and phase relationships
- Room acoustics and playback-system behavior
- Digital signal processing coursework
- Critical comparison of processed and unprocessed audio

## Professional Studio Exposure

My experience at Ocean Way Nashville and Starstruck Entertainment provided exposure to professional recording environments, monitoring systems, session workflows, and the listening decisions required during real productions.

This work reinforced attention to:

- Microphone and source relationships
- Tonal balance
- Imaging and spatial presentation
- Noise, technical problems, and signal integrity
- Communication about audible results
- Consistency within professional studio workflows

## Research and Technical Evaluation

Prior projects connected listening judgments with acoustics and measurement:

- A study examining trained listeners and the detection of frequency-response boosts using loudspeakers versus headphones
- A Dolby Atmos independent study involving immersive microphone technique, spatial imaging, localization, room acoustics, and multichannel reproduction
- Audio Precision testing and technical comparison of Studer tape-machine behavior
- Room-response measurement and acoustic analysis using REW
- DSP work in MATLAB and ongoing audio-analysis development in Python

These experiences provided exposure to both subjective listening and objective verification.

## What This Project Adds

Although I have substantial musical, studio, and academic listening experience, I have not previously documented technical ear training as a continuous, scored development program across these software chapters.

This project adds:

- Repeatable exercise conditions
- Baseline, current, and best scores
- Chapter-by-chapter progression
- Confidence tracking
- Documentation of incorrect judgments
- Analysis of recurring perceptual confusions
- Periodic reflections connecting listening to measurement
- Application to loudspeaker, DSP, and automotive-audio evaluation

## Development Objective

The objective is to make existing listening experience more precise, repeatable, technically descriptive, and demonstrable. Rather than treating a trained ear as an unsupported claim, this repository will show the process and evidence of continued development.
