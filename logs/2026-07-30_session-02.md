## Interpretation and Limitation

These results demonstrate accurate matching when the processed conditions can be compared. They should not be described as memorized frequency identification, unaided recall, or rapid identification. Those are separate and more demanding tasks that require their own exercise settings and evidence.

The two-band and three-band conditions are maintained as separate criteria from each other and from the earlier one-band +6 dB octave-frequency exercise. Their scores are not pooled because the number of simultaneously affected bands changes the task.

## Initial Reflection

The 97% two-band result and 90% three-band result indicate strong comparison-based recognition of multiple +6 dB EQ boosts over pink noise under the tested range, frequency resolution, and Q. The most important documentation point is the method: performance came from matching audible changes rather than identifying the processing solely from an internal memory reference or under a speed requirement.

## Warmup-Matrix Mapping

Today’s 30-trial two-band exercise functions as the transition check in the new daily matrix, and the 10-trial three-band exercise establishes the default multi-frequency warmup condition. Because both met the current 90% boost-matching criterion, no return to one-band practice is needed for routine work; one-band exercises remain available for reference calibration or troubleshooting.

## Training Progression Decision

Based on this session, three-band matching is now the default for future pink-noise boost practice using +6 dB boosts, one-octave center frequencies, Q 2, and a 250 Hz–8 kHz range. One- and two-band conditions may still be revisited for delayed-retention checks, controlled comparisons, or troubleshooting, but routine pink-noise boost practice will progress from the three-band condition. This is a practice-progression decision, not a claim of memorized or rapid-identification mastery. The routine is formalized in the [Daily Critical-Listening Warmup and Progression Sheet](../templates/DAILY_WARMUP_PROGRESSION.md).

## Frequency-Band Work Closeout

Frequency-band work is complete for today. Future pink-noise boost practice will consistently incorporate multiple affected frequencies rather than returning to one-band work as the routine condition.

The planned progression is:

1. Stabilize confidence and accuracy with three-band pink-noise matching.
2. Gradually reduce response time from day to day without sacrificing accuracy or careful listening.
