## Exploratory Work: Compression and Reverb

Compression and reverberation identification exercises were attempted for part of the session. Quiz-style repetition did not yet produce reliable listening cues, so I searched for a method that would allow me to manipulate the processing parameters directly and hear the differences before returning to formal identification questions. These exercises were paused for a later session.

## Training Strategy Note

I plan to assemble a larger and more varied library of auditory samples for future exercises, including music, speech, individual instruments, environmental sounds, and other program material. That sample library has not yet been created, so the current phase focuses on controlled sources such as pink noise and sine waves. These materials make it easier to isolate individual processing changes while I build familiarity with parameters and auditory cues that were not covered, or were not explored in depth, during my university coursework. Once the sample library is ready, I will repeat these exercises across a wider range of source types to test whether the listening skills transfer beyond controlled signals.

## Observations During Session

- Accuracy remained strong when identifying large and moderate EQ boosts at +12 dB and +6 dB. Performance declined as the boost was reduced to +3 dB.
- During the -12 dB cut exercise, attenuated frequencies often made neighboring frequency regions seem unusually prominent. This sometimes led me to select an adjacent frequency rather than the actual cut.
- Hard clipping on pink noise was initially unfamiliar. Recognition improved during the 100 trials as I became more familiar with its audible characteristics.
- Hard clipping on a sine wave produced more consistent cues than pink noise. Settings 2 through 4 became the most reliable range, while settings 1 and 5 remained the most difficult.
- Setting 5 was particularly difficult to hear clearly. Despite that, I developed a good rhythm through much of the middle range and performed well overall.
- Bit-depth reduction on pink noise was substantially more difficult than the clipping exercises. I did not establish a reliable listening strategy during the first 20 trials.
- Compression and reverberation appeared to require exploratory listening and direct parameter adjustment before quiz-style identification would be productive.

## Session Reflection

This session showed that different auditory skills may require different learning approaches. Frequency identification and hard-clipping recognition responded reasonably well to repeated quiz-style practice. Compression and reverberation were more difficult to learn through questions alone and appear better suited to exploratory listening first, followed by formal testing once the relevant cues are understood.

The session also established several useful baselines. Large and moderate EQ boosts were recognized consistently, smaller boosts were more difficult, and cuts created a perceptual contrast effect that could redirect attention toward neighboring bands. Hard clipping became easier with exposure, especially on a sine wave, while bit-depth reduction remained unfamiliar.
